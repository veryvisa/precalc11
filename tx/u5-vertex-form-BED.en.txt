This is the brief on vertex form and transformations. First, to understand a parabola, you've got to know where it starts. That point is the vertex at p comma q. Think of p inside the parentheses as a trickster. If you see x minus 3, your brain naturally thinks go left.

But that trickster actually slides the graph right. Meanwhile, outside the parentheses, q is a straight shooter, sliding the graph up or down exactly as its sign says. Second, so how do we actually draw this without plotting a million random points? Well, the shape is entirely controlled by A.

If A is large, the parabola gets tall and skinny. If A is negative, the whole thing just flips upside down. To sketch it fast, use the step pattern. From the vertex, go over 1, 2, and 3 units. Then up by 1A, 4A, and 9A for an instant visual map.
