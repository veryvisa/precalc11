This is the brief on solving radical equations and extraneous roots. First, stick to a non-negotiable four-step process. Start by stating your restrictions, because the stuff under an even root must be 0 or positive. Then, isolate the radical on one side. Next, raise both sides to match the index, like squaring for a square root.

Finally, solve and check every single answer in the original equation. Well, second, beware the extraneous root. This is a total fake-out, an answer solving your squared equation but failing the original. Squaring literally loses the negative sign. 2 doesn't equal negative 2, right?

But square both sides and suddenly 4 equals 4. It's a fake. Graphically, these extraneous roots never show up as intersections, because a principal root curve can't meet a negative number. Finally, let's avoid some common pitfalls.

Don't square before isolating the radical. Got two radicals? Isolate one, square both sides, then repeat for the second. Squaring a binomial? Don't forget that middle term.

Also, don't miss impossible equations. A principal square root is never negative. If you see the square root of x equals negative 4, stop right there. There's no solution. Isolate your radicals, square with caution, and absolutely always check your work in the original equation

to weed out those extraneous roots.
